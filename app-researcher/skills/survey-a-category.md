# Surveying a category

## Picking the apps

- Start from the store's own ranking for the category on the platform and country the task names (on the App Store, its chart for the genre, `https://itunes.apple.com/<cc>/rss/topfreeapplications/limit=50/genre=<genre-id>/json`, and its search for the category's words), then lists and reviews by publications that test apps; take the apps most of them name, and a few newer or niche ones that do something the leaders do not, since charts surface only the popular. {#start-from-rankings}
- Add a row for what the category's users do instead of an app where the survey shows they do (the phone's built-in app, a spreadsheet, paper), read as far as a visitor can; it is what a first version competes with too. {#count-the-alternatives}
- Take as many apps as the task asks; where it names none, about eight, and say that you chose the number and how you picked them. {#how-many-apps}
- Write down each app's store URL first: the store page is the anchor every later claim about the app points back to. On the App Store, the store's own lookup, `https://itunes.apple.com/lookup?id=<id>&country=<cc>`, gives as JSON its price, rating, rating count, version and release date, read anonymously; cite it beside the page, and keep under Apple's limit of about 20 calls a minute. {#store-url-first}

## Reading each app the same way

One row per app, every column filled or marked "not found", each value with its URL:

- what it is, in one line, and for whom, as its store page says; {#row-what-and-for-whom}
- price: free, paid, subscription, in-app purchases, and what the free tier holds, as the page lists it; {#row-price}
- rating and how many ratings, with the country and the date you read them, since both move and each store country keeps its own; a developer may reset the rating at a new version, so where the current version's count is far below the app's age, say the rating covers that version only; {#row-rating}
- its main features, as the page or its own site lists them, said in your words; {#row-features}
- what reviewers praise and what they complain of, read from at least the most recent and the most helpful reviews and one article, counted ("most of the 30 recent reviews read…") and summarised, never quoted at length; on the App Store, the reviews feed, `https://itunes.apple.com/<cc>/rss/customerreviews/page=<1-10>/id=<id>/sortby=mostrecent/json`, gives 50 a page with each one's stars, date and version, so count by version too: a complaint only of versions an update replaced is a past one; {#row-praise-and-complaints}
- platforms, and when it was last updated. {#row-platforms-updated}

## The source log

- Keep a log as you read, one line per page: URL, date read, what it gave. Every claim in the survey names a line of it by its URL; a claim you cannot point back to is cut. {#source-log}
- Prefer the source that owns the fact: the app's store page or its own site for what it offers and costs, the store for ratings, a dated article or the reviews for what users find. A write-up of a write-up is cited only with the source it cites. {#source-that-owns-it}
- A page that will not load without signing in, paying or installing is not read: it goes into the log as not read, and why. {#not-read-is-logged}

## Across the category

After the rows, what the category shares and where it differs: features nearly every app has (the table stakes), features only a few have, the complaints that come back across apps, and the gaps no app fills. Each of these is a finding, numbered, citing the rows it rests on.
