# Talking to workers

Every word between you and a worker is a comment on its task's issue; the
scheduler types it into whom it is for. Say what the worker needs to act,
in the task's own terms, and end your turn: do not wait or poll.

- **Answer a question.** A stuck worker asks with `guild task question`; it is typed into your session with the task and the question's id. Answer from the goal, its tasks and their issues: `guild task answer <task> <question> --answer "<text>"`; the worker wakes with it. Answer what was asked, within the task's scope and acceptance: never widen the scope or change an acceptance item in an answer. When the answer changes what the goal needs, change the split instead (a re-split of queued tasks, or a task added: skills/pull-a-lever.md). A running worker that asks to go past its scope has seen what the split missed: answer that a re-split comes, soft-stop it, and re-split once from all it found (skills/pull-a-lever.md), never a running task under it. When only Felix can say (product direction, money), say so in the answer, and that the worker asks him with `guild task ask`. Never promise to tell a worker later (once a task lands, say): your session wakes only on what reaches it. When the answer is what another task makes, answer with `--wait <that task>`: the asker's task waits on it, queued, and runs again once it lands; never answer "wait" without it, for a worker cannot wait inside its turn and its task fails. When a task cannot go on until other work lands, say so on the goal: what it waits for and whose move that is (skills/pull-a-lever.md). {#answer-a-question}
- **Redirect a worker.** When a working worker goes the wrong way (another task's paths, a design the goal rules out, work the task does not ask), `guild task tell <task> --message "<text>"`: it takes it once its current turn ends. Say what to change and why, briefly. A message for a task no instance works is kept among its constraints for the next one. {#redirect-a-worker}
- **Soft-stop a worker.** When a task's work must stop before it goes on (its goal changed under it, it repeats a failing approach after you told it, or it is to be re-split), find its instance with `guild status` and `guild stop <instance> --reason "<why>"`: it stops before its next tool call, its session kept. Continue it with `guild resume <instance> "<instruction>"` once you know what it does next, or re-split its task. A stop exits 1 when no tool call came within its wait: say so on the task; never stop it hard. A worker that breaks the rules is the warden's to hard-stop. {#soft-stop-a-worker}
