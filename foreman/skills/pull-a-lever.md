# Pulling a lever while a goal runs

Once your split is accepted your session is kept. When a phase of one of
the goal's tasks is far slower than usual, its flow report so far is typed
into it: lead time, time by phase with why, the critical path. Act on it,
or on your own reading of the goal; then end.

Each lever is a request on the goal; the scheduler carries it out by its
rules, says it on the goal, keeps it in L3, and the next flow report shows
what changed. Each command waits for the answer: 0 once carried out, 1
with the reason it was refused.

- Priority and order, to move the critical path first: `guild goal lever <goal> priority <task> high|normal|low`; `guild goal lever <goal> order <task> --before <task>`, the second, still queued, then waits on the first. {#priority-and-order-to-move-the}
- Merge or re-split tasks still queued, or failed, never one running or in review, save the pair the scheduler hands back once a verification failed: read each one's `state/` label first, and its verification task's, which you replace with it. One still running you soft-stop (skills/talk-to-workers.md) or let settle before you propose; one in another state that is not yours to change waits, said on the goal. Propose a split file with `replaces: [<task>, …]`, with `guild goal split <goal> <file>`. The new tasks pass the split rules, side by side with the tasks the goal keeps too; the replaced ones are closed, saying which replaced them, and what waited on them waits on the new ones. {#merge-or-re-split-tasks-still}
- A task that fails, or is sent back, on what no worker of it can change (a repo not found, so it never starts; an item whose check needs a credential nobody is given, so it never passes; a scope too narrow for the change its items need) fails again as it is, however often it is requeued; the scheduler hands one that cannot start back to you, naming the repos the forge has. Re-split it so it can start and pass, keeping every piece of the goal it held: a piece that cannot start or be checked yet you leave out and name on the goal, with what it waits for and whose move that is. {#a-task-that-fails-or-is-sent}
- Re-split a task once, so the new one is the last. A re-split that mends the one fact just learnt, then another for the next, goes back and forth: each closes work under way and restarts it. Spend it only on what your split got wrong and only a re-split mends; a task held by the steward, or waiting on another goal's work that moves or is itself re-split, is a wait, not a re-split: say it on the goal, and its worker reads where things stand once it runs. Then the one re-split is still there for what needs it, a verification that failed, say. Before you propose, gather all that bears on it: the worker's findings and open questions (ask it what else stands between it and every item, and wait for the answer), holds and their reasons, what the steward or the trainer crew asked. Then walk each item to true, from its start through landing and release: the repo clones, the scope holds every path the change needs, the check runs with what its checker is known to hold (skills/split-a-goal.md), and an item true only after landing or release is marked so and has someone who lands or releases it, and one true only after the session that no mark covers is left to the goal (skills/split-a-goal.md). A gap no task of yours closes is said on the goal, not re-split around. {#re-split-a-task-once-so-the-new}
- A queued task's time says why it waits (`guild flow <goal>`); each why is someone's move. One that waits on another task waits on that task's why: follow the chain to the task that holds it, and act on its why. Behind the goal's other tasks: yours, by priority or order. A role not landed: the trainer crew's. Passed review and not landed, with none ahead in the landing queue: the landing's, so the steward's; no landing mode moves it. The goal's own priority (low work waits for an idle machine): Felix's; never raise a task past it to beat the wait. Held by the steward: its hold says what it waits for; follow that. Machines full for long: a money ask, below. No scheduler round, readings missing or the Hub lost: the steward's. Over a wait that is not yours, never re-split, re-prioritize or ask for machines: say on the goal what holds it, and whose move it is; a goal left unable to move with nothing said stalls until someone notices. {#a-queued-task-s-time-says-why-it}
- The landing mode of the goal's repo: `guild goal lever <goal> landing <repo> batch|per-candidate|declared`, for this goal alone; it switches back on its own once the goal ends. A batch round gates only the candidate chain's tip: worth it when many small changes wait to land. Switch it once, on what a flow report shows, and keep it for the goal; never back and forth. {#the-landing-mode-of-the-goal-s}
- Fix what is slow with work, as any work: `replaces: []` adds a task to the goal (a slow test suite, a gate to split, a cache to share); work beyond the goal is a goal of its own (`guild goal "<title>"`). {#fix-what-is-slow-with-work-as}
- More machines or quota spends money: `guild goal lever <goal> more machines|quota --why "<what the flow report shows>"` asks Felix, a decision ticket. Never add them yourself. {#more-machines-or-quota-spends}
