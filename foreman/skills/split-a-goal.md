# Splitting a goal

The scheduler starts you on a goal with a brief: the goal, the roles lore has,
the roles drafted in lore's open pull requests, and the split rules.

- Spec the goal before you cut it, short, from the goal and its repos alone, after Matt Pocock's to-spec: nobody is interviewed. The problem and the solution as whoever asked sees them; the decisions it takes (modules, interfaces, contracts, data shapes), in the repo's glossary terms and true to its ADRs; how it is tested, at the highest seam the repo has, with the fewest new ones; and what is out of scope. Say it on the goal. Each task's objective, constraints and acceptance come from it, and what it rules out is in no task. {#spec-the-goal-before-you-cut-it}
- Question the spec against each repo's ADRs and GLOSSARY (read from its main) before you cut it, in place of Matt Pocock's grill-with-docs, in rounds: first the questions whose prerequisites are settled, each with your recommended answer, then those the answers open, until none is left. Nobody is grilled and Felix is not asked: each question is decided by its recommended answer, which keeps to what the ADRs decided and the terms the glossary defines, and the decision is written into the tasks it bears on, as a constraint or an acceptance item. A fact is looked up in the repo, never put to a worker. Where the goal asks against an ADR, or uses a term against the glossary's meaning, name the conflict on the goal: the ADR or term, what the goal asks, and how the split decided it. {#question-the-spec-against-each-repo}
- Split into as many tasks as the goal needs, and no more: the fewest that keep side-by-side work apart. No rule limits how many; the machines bound how many run at once. A small goal is one task. {#split-into-as-many-tasks-as-the}
- Split by independent artifacts: two tasks that may run side by side change different paths. A coupled design is one task, or tasks in order (`after`). A task `after` another starts only once that one lands, not once it passes review: order tasks only when the later one needs the earlier one's change landed. A task `after` another that changes the same paths could be that task's own work: merge them, or say in its `apart` why they cannot be one (a review that must land first, a different role's work); the split rules refuse one that says none. Ask the same of every two tasks: why not one? {#split-by-independent-artifacts}
- Cut each task as a tracer bullet, after Matt Pocock's to-tickets: a narrow but whole path through every layer its piece touches (data, logic, interface, tests), which its checks show working on its own, sized to one instance's budget; never one layer left for a later task to wire up. Its blocking edges are its `after`: the tasks whose landed change it needs, and no others; a task with none starts now. A prefactor that makes the slices easy is a task of its own, first. A wide mechanical change (a rename or retype across a codebase) that no slice can land green is the exception: expand (the new form beside the old), then migrate in batches sized by blast radius, each `after` the expand, then contract (drop the old form) `after` every batch. Say each task's objective as the behaviour it makes work, in the repo's glossary terms, not as files: its scope says where. {#cut-each-task-as-a-tracer-bullet}
- Split only work that can start now. Scope each task only in repos the forge has: a task in a repo the scheduler cannot clone fails before it starts. Check every repo of the split with `git ls-remote <forge>/guild/<repo>.git` first (the `origin` of lore shows the forge); the brief's example repo is an example, not a repo you have. Work that builds on what has not landed (another goal's task: `after` names only tasks of the same split) cannot start either: a constraint telling the worker to wait only parks it and fails the task. Leave such a piece out of the split, a repo to be made (`guild repo new`, on the Hub, not yours to run) or work of another goal, and say on the goal what it waits for and whose move that is; split it once it has landed. {#split-only-work-that-can-start}
- Give each task a `priority` within the goal's: the critical path highest, work that can wait lower. A low goal's tasks are low: they wait for an idle machine or a spare account, hours on a busy one; the goal's priority is Felix's, never raised by a task's. {#give-each-task-a-priority-within}
- Give each task a role lore has, or one drafted in lore. When none fits, ask for the role: list it under `requests` (its `role`, the `work` it does, its `constraints`) and give the task that role. The scheduler opens the role request as a task for the trainer crew; the task waits until the role lands. {#give-each-task-a-role-lore-has}
- While lore has no warden, the split is refused: the trainer drafts it first. {#while-lore-has-no-warden-the}
- Give every acceptance item its check, how it is verified: a `command` and what it must show, which the warden reruns; a `ui` walk-through and what must be seen; or a `link`. An item with none refuses the split. An item that comes true only after the work lands (a tool in forge's index, one that runs by its name once released) is marked `after: landed`, or `after: released`, beside its check: its worker leaves it, the work lands without it, and a follow-up of the task checks it; without the mark the task fails however well it is done. One true only after the worker's session ends that no mark covers (a replay or a measure run on its pull request) is no task's item: the task settles when its session ends, and an item still todo fails it; say it on the goal as what the goal waits for, and whose move it is. {#give-every-acceptance-item-its}
- A check runs with what its checker is given: its repo, fixtures, its own account. One that needs a credential, an account or a machine no worker, warden or verifier of the task is given (a read-only token for a live service, say) never passes: the task fails or is sent back however good its work, and naming another checker (`by: "warden"`) helps only when that one holds it. Know it from whoever hands credentials out (the steward), asked and answered, never from a suggestion, a role's name or a hope: an assumed holder fails the task in review, and re-splitting it then goes back and forth. Check what can be checked now (on fixtures), and leave what needs the missing credential out of the split, as a piece that cannot start yet: a credential is a person's to make, so say on the goal what it waits for and that it is Felix's or the steward's move. {#a-check-runs-with-what-its}
- A check that needs more than a command the warden can rerun, a UI tried for real or a recording, gets a verification task of its own: after the change alone, its `verifies` naming the items it walks, for a verification role other than the change's (a UI tester; ask for it when none fits, with the capabilities it needs), changing nothing the change changes. It hands in evidence; the warden judges it in the change's review. {#a-check-that-needs-more-than-a}
- When questioning leaves decisions nothing known now settles (a fact to research, a prototype to react to, work that must be done before a choice can be judged), chart a map of decisions on the goal, after Matt Pocock's wayfinder, in place of cutting the whole goal. Name its destination (the spec, decision or change the goal finds its way to). Split now only what you can state sharply: one decision task per question, each sized to one instance, its item a `link` to where its answer is written (an ADR in the repo where the choice is hard to reverse, surprising without context and a real trade-off; else its task's issue), `after` where one gates another; and the building tasks already clear. What is in scope but too foggy to phrase sharply stays on the goal as not yet specified; what is past the destination, as out of scope; neither is split. {#chart-a-map-of-decisions}
- Resolve the map one decision at a time: as each decision task lands, say its answer on the goal in one line beside the task (decisions so far), then add the tasks it made specifiable (`replaces: []`), take what it showed past the destination out of scope, and let the rest of the fog wait for the next answer. The map is done when nothing is left to decide; what remains is building, cut as tracer bullets. {#resolve-the-map-one-decision-at-a}

Write the split to a file and run `guild goal split <goal> <file>`. It exits 0
once the scheduler opened the tasks, and 1 with every reason it refused them:
change the split and propose it again. Once it is accepted, say on the goal
what any task of it waits for before it can run (an idle machine, a role,
another task) and whose move that is; then end.
