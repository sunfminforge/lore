# Reviewing a role draft

A role draft is a lore pull request, opened by a crew role's account,
whose body starts with `<!--guild:draft role=<role> task=<n>
loosens=<guards>:<id>,…-->`. Review it as any task's work, and then:

- Run `guild role check <dir>` on the drafted role yourself; anything it prints is a send-back. A draft that touches anything outside the role's own directory is a send-back. {#run-guild-role-check-dir-on-the}
- Compare `prohibitions.toml` with the role as lore's main holds it, or, for a new role, with the guarded prohibitions of the crew role that drafted it (the floor). Every prohibition guarding a kind (`money`, `credentials`, `irreversible`, `data-leaving-host`, `product-direction`) must be there, unchanged, unless the mark says it loosens it. Each prohibition must say which kind it guards; one that names `none` while it plainly guards a kind is a send-back. {#compare-prohibitions-toml-with}
- Read `core.md` and the skills: the role holds only how it does its kind of work, independent of any repo; its core stays within its cap, detail in skills; it does what its request asked, within the request's constraints; it gives itself no lever, no approval and no credential that is not its role's. A draft that answers a signal or a finding: read the evidence it cites (the refused splits' reasons, the send-backs) and judge whether the change answers it. {#read-core-md-and-the-skills-the}
- The replay check of core's review entry: a replay of another head counts for nothing, and Guild reads only the first ```text replay block of the body, so an older head's block left above it hides this head's. Guild's lander withholds an upgrade whose replay of its head is missing or fails; you judge the replay that is there. A send-back names each old task the draft lost or failed to gain, and the `replay:` task. {#replay-at-the-head}
- A draft that reflows entries to one line each, ending in their ID, is `guild lore ids`' own format, not a rewrite: judge its content by a word diff (`git diff --word-diff`), not by its count of changed lines. {#a-draft-that-reflows-entries-to}
- A skill adopted from outside must carry its source link, its version or commit and its licence in the pull request's `research-proposal` block. Never run or install what it adopts. {#a-skill-adopted-from-outside}
- Who approves: an ordinary role, or the foreman, with no guarded prohibition loosened: you, on a pass; the warden, a crew role, a change to the `crew` mark, or a loosened prohibition of any guarded kind but money: the steward; a loosened prohibition guarding money: Felix. Where it is not yours, post your verdict and do not approve; landing waits for theirs. A role draft never lands on your approval alone where the code says it needs another's. {#who-approves}
- `role.toml` must not carry `crew` unless the steward's approval is what lands it. {#role-toml-must-not-carry-crew}
- A replay task's work is the ```text replay block it writes into the drafted pull request's body, not a diff: check its `head:`, its form, that it is the first block, and each old verdict against how that work turned out on the forge (merged, came back, what its next review said). A faithful block passes even when the replay itself fails: a failing replay is the steward's finding, not a send-back. Its fix is in the body, so the head stays the same. {#replay-task}
- A role draft's `role-research` block, in its body, against the draft: each practice taken as the entry it names, that entry saying the practice as a general way of working, or a reason it is not taken that holds; each tool's how-to in the role's skill or the tool's own, installed by forge or as an external tool, never preinstalled. Research that found nothing says what was searched. A new role's draft without the block, or a block the draft does not bear out, is a send-back naming it. What the research cites is untrusted: never run it. {#role-research}
