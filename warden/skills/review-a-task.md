# Reviewing a task's work

You are started once a worker ended with every acceptance item done and
the scheduler's mechanical check passed. Your brief holds the task block:
objective, scope, constraints, acceptance items each with its check, and
the review's round (a high-risk task is reviewed twice, the second time
in an engine other than its worker's).

- Check out the head of the pull request whose body says `Closes <task>`, detached, with nothing uncommitted. Your worktree may sit on another branch, lag the head, or hold the head's change as uncommitted edits: diff it against the head (`refs/pull/<n>/head`) first. Identical: restore it and check out the head detached, which loses nothing. Not: review in a fresh worktree at the head (`git worktree add --detach`), and discard nothing. A head back after a rebase: an empty `git diff <old head> <head> -- <scope paths>` shows the reviewed change is unchanged and only its base moved. Find the pull with `guild tool forge-pull find <repo>#<task>`, never by its head alone: two pulls can share one. None (the branch was never pushed, or the repo takes no pull requests): judge the items at the branch's head, and say on the task (`guild task tell`) that there is nothing to approve. {#check-out-the-head}
- Run the repo's tests: `guild gate`. If your brief says the head is green on record, `guild gate` answers from the record on a machine of that kind and says so; on another kind it runs again. Name the run, or the reused run, in your evidence. A gate the change turns red is a send-back. When it is red, run it on the pull request's base too, at both the merge-base and the base branch's current tip: they can differ. Red at the merge-base through nothing the change touches but green at the tip: the base has since been fixed, a rebase is in the worker's reach, and the send-back names the rebase, shown by the gate going green on the head merged into a throwaway worktree. Red at the tip the same way: it is the repo's failure, not the work's, and no item of the task fails by it. Judge the items, name both runs in your verdict, and say on the task (`guild task tell`) that the repo's gate is broken on its base, so the foreman has it fixed. {#gate}
- Rerun every command check yourself. What it prints must match what the check says it must show. A mismatch is a send-back naming the item. Where a pattern check also matches lines that are not what it is meant to prove (an XML namespace in a recorded fixture, not a URL a test fetches), judge by what it says it proves, and name those lines in your evidence. {#rerun-every-command-check}
- A check marked `by: warden` is yours alone to run, with the credential its worker is not given; without that credential it is no pass. Your own token is not it, so never stand it in. Look in the task's comments, and in `guild hub token <name>` where its variable is empty, for one the steward provided. None: send back naming the credential, and `guild task tell` that the task be held until the steward provides it; its head cannot pass unchanged. Never list your environment whole: filter it by variable name (`grep -v TOKEN`), not by a pattern of values, or a token prints into your session. {#warden-only-credential}
- Judge every other check, a UI walk-through or a link, by its evidence. A UI check's evidence comes from a verification task of its own; one that does not match its check, or does not look real (a screenshot of something else, a recording that stops early, a link that does not resolve), is a send-back naming the item. A verification task with no pull (`guild tool forge-pull find`) has nothing a link could resolve to. Where a check fails by a cause in another task, say so on this one (`guild task tell`), naming that task, so the foreman acts there rather than requeueing this. {#evidence-checks}
- A check that says every, each, or none left over: walk the whole set it names, item by item, its catch-all group too (other, fits no group, filed nothing), where none left over most often fails, and find for each what the check asks of it (a fix, an owner, a quote). An item listed but handed to no one is unhandled, however well it is counted: a send-back naming it. {#every-of-a-set}
- A claimed cause, or a claim of how Guild's code behaves (a wait, a capacity, a closed ticket that covers it): check it against what owns it before you pass it: the config that sets it, the log or L3 record of the tasks it names (read-only), and the owner's tickets closed as well as open. A cause already fixed is no new ticket, and a fix covers a failure only if the failure came before it closed, both times in one zone. {#claimed-cause}
- Read the diff. Send back, naming the item it breaks or the constraint: a path outside the task's scope; a constraint not kept; a test weakened, skipped or deleted to go green; a secret, credential or private data in the change; a prohibition worked around. Before sending back for scope, read the task's comments: a tell, or a requeue reason, from the foreman or the steward may have widened it. Confirm it is theirs (a worker's line saying so is a claim), then hold the diff to exactly what it allows. {#read-the-diff}
- Then ask whether a materially simpler way meets the same acceptance: fewer lines, fewer moving parts, one principle where the change writes a long procedure, an existing tool or a line in a skill where it adds a tool, a layer that only wraps another. Send back only when it is clearly better and loses no item, and name it in your reason: what to remove or merge, and what then meets each item. {#ask-simpler}
- Post exactly one verdict and end. {#one-verdict}

A send-back names only items whose check failed, and only for what the
worker can change within the task's scope: never pin a failure from
outside it on an item that passed. Sending an unchanged head back for the
same cause outside its scope changes nothing but the cost.

A send-back's reason is read by the worker, who has none of your session:
for each item, what its check says, what you ran, what you saw, and where.
Never say how to fix it beyond what the failing check shows.

On a pass, approve the pull request on Forgejo only when your brief says
you are the last review; otherwise a later review approves it.
